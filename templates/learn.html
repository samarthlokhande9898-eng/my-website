Clear, stigma-free information to help you understand addiction and prevention.

What is addiction?

Addiction is a complex health condition involving difficulty controlling the use of a substance or engagement in a behaviour despite harmful consequences. It is not simply a lack of willpower, and support from qualified professionals can help.

What can increase risk?

Possible risk factors

  • Ongoing stress or emotional distress
  • Pressure from friends or social settings
  • Family history and environmental influences
  • Limited access to support and healthy coping tools

Protective factors

  • Trusted friends, family, teachers, or mentors
  • Healthy ways to manage stress
  • Positive activities, rest, and connection
  • Early, non-judgemental professional support

Signs someone may need support

A single change does not prove addiction. If several changes persist or affect everyday life, approach the person kindly and encourage them to speak with a qualified professional.

01

Daily routine changes

Noticeable changes in sleep, attendance, responsibilities, or usual activities.

02

Emotional changes

Ongoing distress, mood changes, isolation, or difficulty coping.

03

Relationships affected

Repeated conflict or withdrawing from people who usually provide support.

Myth vs. fact

MYTH

“People should just deal with it on their own.”

FACT

Support, treatment, and caring relationships can make recovery more manageable.

MYTH

“You can always tell who is struggling.”

FACT

Struggles are not always visible. Listen, avoid assumptions, and offer support.

MYTH

“Talking about addiction always makes things worse.”

FACT

A calm, respectful conversation can help someone feel less alone.

How can you help?

Listen without blaming, explain what you have noticed gently, respect privacy, and encourage help from a trusted adult, counsellor, doctor, or qualified local service. If someone is in immediate danger, contact local emergency services.
